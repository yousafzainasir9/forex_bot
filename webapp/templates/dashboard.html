▶ How this bot works (click to expand)

The bot watches one currency pair (default EUR/USD) and checks it once every 15 minutes. Each check it does one of four things: BUY, SELL, CLOSE, or HOLD (do nothing). It decides using three simple tools stacked together:

1. Direction — two moving averages crossing

A moving average is the average price over the last N bars, redrawn each bar as a smooth line. The bot tracks a fast line (9 bars) and a slow line (21 bars). Fast crossing above slow = price turning up → a BUY idea. Crossing below = turning down → a SELL idea.

2. Momentum filter — RSI

RSI is a 0–100 gauge of how stretched price is. Above 70 = "overbought" (already ran up a lot); below 30 = "oversold" (already fell a lot). The bot only BUYs if RSI is below 70, and only SELLs if RSI is above 30 — so it doesn't chase a move that may be exhausted.

3. Safety net — ATR-based stop & target

ATR measures the pair's typical move per bar (its "normal wiggle"). Every trade gets a stop-loss (auto-close if it goes against you) 1.5×ATR away, and a take-profit 1.5× further than the stop — aiming to earn 1.5× what a loser costs (a 1 : 1.5 risk/reward).

The bot also exits early if the lines cross back the other way, and otherwise just HOLDs — sitting out is a deliberate choice.

In one line: buy when the trend turns up and isn't overbought, sell when it turns down and isn't oversold, always with a protective stop, and otherwise wait.

Money rules

Risks 1% of the account per trade (position size is computed so a stop-out costs ~1%), keeps at most 1 trade open, and halts for the day if losses reach 3% of the day's starting balance.

⚠️ This is a deliberately simple learning strategy on a DEMO account — not a proven money-maker. Hover any figure below for its definition.
